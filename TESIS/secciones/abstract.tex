\begin{center}
\large \vspace{-1.5cm} \textbf{Urban traffic optimization in Lima with intelligent traffic signals through Reinforcement Learning (RL) and SUMO simulation}
\end{center}

Lima Metropolitana has critical congestion points at high-traffic intersections and corridors. This thesis asks whether a traffic signal controller that learns with \textbf{Reinforcement Learning (RL)} and receives information from its neighbouring signals reduces the \textbf{delay per vehicle} with respect to a well-tuned fixed-time program, without falling behind actuated control, the two usual references in traffic engineering.

A test bench was built in \textbf{SUMO} with three synthetic networks (a two-signal corridor, a three-signal network with a roundabout and a nine-signal grid), each under nominal and high demand: six cases in total. The agents, one per signal, and the message exchange between neighbours (IPPO with messages) were programmed from scratch and compared with IPPO without messages, MAPPO, Double DQN, Q-learning and SARSA. Before evaluating, the protocol (three training runs of 200 episodes per model, 30 evaluation seeds, confidence intervals and Wilcoxon tests) and a verdict rule for each model in each case were fixed.

Under that rule, IPPO with messages reduces delay with respect to the tuned fixed-time program in the corridor and in the roundabout network (by 3.6 to 23.3\%), ties with it in the grid (provisionally under high demand) and has less delay than actuated control in all six cases. Summarised in a single number, the change is $-6.83$\% against fixed time and $-14.35$\% against actuated control. Except in the corridor under high demand, what separates working from failing is the neighbour's information: without messages, the same neural network fails in the roundabout network and in the grid and, summarised in a single number, stays 1.35\% above fixed time; Q-learning and SARSA lose to it in every nominal-demand case.

The improvement falls short of the target of a 25--40\% delay reduction, which is not reached in any case, and of the published median against a tuned fixed-time, Webster or field plan ($-35.7$\%), because the reference is well tuned. Under a stricter measurement decided after the verdict, which adds the wait to enter the network and tunes fixed time from the minimum green, the advantage over fixed time holds clearly only in the corridor under nominal demand and the summary number becomes $-0.03$\%; against actuated control it holds ($-11.45$\%). With regular arrivals, a well-tuned fixed-time program is nearly optimal. The real intersection in Lima was imported from OpenStreetMap but not evaluated.
